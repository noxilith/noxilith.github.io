EloraNotes privacy policy

Last updated 28 October 2026.

EloraNotes is a notes app for the desktop and the iPhone. It was made so that your notes stay yours. This page says exactly what the iPhone app stores and what it sends.

What the developer collects

Nothing. The app has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising, no crash reporting, no tracking and no third-party code that talks to the internet. Nothing you write, and nothing about how you use the app, is sent to the developer or to anyone else on the developer's behalf.

What is stored on your phone

  • Your notes are plain Markdown files in the EloraNotes folder of the Files app, on your phone. You can copy, back up or delete them like any other files. Deleting the app deletes them.
  • Settings (your theme, which folders you collapsed, whether previews show) are stored in the app's settings on your phone.
  • Shared library login, if you set one up: the server address, your name, and the server password and identifier. The password and identifier are kept in the iOS Keychain.

The shared library (optional)

If you connect the app to a shared library, the app sends your notes, your name and your caret position to the server you entered, and receives the notes and carets of the people you share with. That server is run by you or by someone you chose, not by the developer, and what it does with the data is up to whoever runs it. The connection is encrypted, and the app only talks to a server whose certificate you confirmed when you connected. If you never set up a shared library, nothing leaves your phone.

iOS asks for Local Network access the first time the app connects, because a shared library is often a server in your home or office. That permission is used only to reach the server you entered.

Children

The app is not directed at children and collects no personal information from anyone.

Changes

If this policy changes it will be updated on this page, with a new date at the top.

Contact

Questions or requests: github.com/noxilith/EloraNotes/issues. The app is open source, so everything above can be checked in the code: github.com/noxilith/EloraNotes.
